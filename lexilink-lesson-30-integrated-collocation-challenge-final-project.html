A Student Team Completes an Evidence-Based Project

A. On 15 September 2025, a fictional university launched a final-project module called Words in Action. Tutor Dr Nadia Karim asked 96 students to select a community issue and conduct research before proposing a response. The goal was to develop competence in using collocations across academic and professional contexts, not simply to memorise word lists.

B. One group chose public transport and access to education. After gathering interviews and checking local reports, they decided to evaluate evidence before making claims. Their first draft included several literal translations; tutor feedback asked them to provide reliable evidence for their claims and to revise a proposal when the wording did not fit the context.

C. A second group focused on online services and privacy. They recommended clearer consent notices and ways to protect personal data. Their team discussion exposed different views about convenience and risk. Rather than forcing agreement, members compared options and tried to reach a consensus on realistic recommendations, while agreeing to manage expectations about what a student project could change.

D. The third group investigated how a new pedestrian route might support local trade. It suggested ways to support local businesses and reduce environmental impact. During the presentation on 12 December 2025, the students present findings using examples and explained how they had used SkELL to test whether selected collocations were natural in context.

E. The module report, published on 20 January 2026, said that 82 of the 96 students submitted a final project by the deadline. Tutors noted improvements in collocation choice, but warned that one module could not prove long-term gains. Students were asked to reflect on progress, identify further priorities and take responsibility for continuing independent practice after the course ended.

IELTS-style Reading Task 3 — Multiple Choice

Choose the best answer according to the passage.

1. Matching · 10 points

Complete each expression using its meaning and the context; choose the word that makes a natural collocation.

2. Find the Collocate · 10 points

Choose the missing word in each collocation. Read the whole expression, not just the blank.

3. Complete the Sentence · 10 points

Use sentence meaning and grammar to select a collocation that fits the context.

4. Multiple Choice · 10 points

Choose the collocation that best fits the situation about integrated collocation challenge and final project, cultural identity or cooperation. Use the contextual meaning to decide; do not classify parts of speech.

5. Lexical Cloze · 10 points

Choose the expression that fits the situation and the meaning of the sentence.

Record two collocations, write an original sentence for each, add related expressions and explain how corpus evidence confirmed or changed your first hypothesis.

7. Odd One Out · Based on SkELL · 10 points

Three options in each question express similar meanings. Choose the one collocation that does NOT belong to the synonym group. All options are natural expressions; the odd one out is different in meaning.

8. Collocation Meaning Check · 10 points

Choose the collocation that matches the meaning described. All options are natural English expressions; use meaning rather than parts of speech.

9. Progressive Lexical Tests · 24 practice points

These tests become progressively harder: recognise collocations, choose expressions in context, compare close meanings, and evaluate realistic situations involving integrated collocation challenge and final project. The practice score is reported separately from the 115-point lesson total.

10. Productive Recall Challenge · 5 points (teacher-scored)

Write at least 150 words for your final project. Choose one issue from your course, use at least 10 target collocations from different topic areas, justify three choices with context or SkELL evidence, and reflect on revisions. The 100-word target is recommended for full writing credit, but unfinished work can still be submitted.

Writing prompt
