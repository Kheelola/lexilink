From Local Traditions to Global Partnerships

A. On 12 March 2024, the fictional city of Northbridge opened a six-month “Shared Streets” programme to help residents understand how global connections affect local life. Project coordinator Maya Rahman said the aim was not to make every neighbourhood alike, but to help people bridge differences while protecting cultural heritage. The programme began after a survey of 420 residents found that 38% had taken part in an international cultural event during the previous year.

B. Northbridge is home to communities whose families arrived from 27 countries. Many residents exchange ideas at food markets, libraries and language cafés. Yet organisers found that a language barrier sometimes prevented newcomers from accessing local services. From April, the council added translated information sheets and volunteer interpreters at three community centres. By June, attendance at the weekly advice sessions had risen from 46 to 71 visits, although the report cautioned that seasonal changes might also have affected participation.

C. Local businesses also depend on global supply chains. On 8 May, the Northbridge Traders’ Association held a workshop on cross-border trade and economic integration. Small business owner Daniel Okafor explained that a delayed shipment of coffee equipment had affected six cafés. He argued that international links could create opportunities, but that businesses needed alternative suppliers and the ability to adapt to change. Participants agreed that cooperation worked best when partners understood each other’s expectations.

D. The programme’s cultural festival took place on 22 June 2024. Organisers invited residents to present music, crafts and stories connected with their backgrounds. Rather than treating culture as something fixed, the event showed how communities can preserve traditions while developing new ones. Historian Dr Leila Chen noted that cultural identity can include several influences at once. She also urged visitors to challenge stereotypes by asking people about their experiences instead of assuming that one person speaks for an entire community.

E. In a report published on 30 September 2024, the council said that 312 people had attended at least one programme activity and 84 volunteers had contributed time. The report described the project as a step towards stronger social cohesion and more international cooperation between local organisations. It recommended continuing translated support and creating more opportunities to promote inclusion. The council did not claim that the programme had caused every positive change; it called for a longer evaluation to discover which activities had the greatest effect and whether the benefits were mutually beneficial for different groups.

7. Odd One Out · Based on SkELL · 10 points

Three options in each question express similar meanings. Choose the one collocation that does NOT belong to the synonym group. All options are natural expressions; the odd one out is different in meaning.
