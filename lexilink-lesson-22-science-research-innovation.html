From Research Question to Scientific Contribution

A research project often begins with a question about a problem that deserves closer attention. Before collecting new information, a researcher should conduct research on what is already known and review the literature to understand previous findings. This process can reveal an unanswered question or address a research gap. The researcher may then formulate a hypothesis that can be examined through an appropriate method. A clear plan helps the team decide what information to collect and how to reduce avoidable bias.

The next stage is to collect data systematically and analyse data using methods suited to the research question. Researchers need to test an assumption, check alternative explanations and interpret results carefully. Evidence should support a claim; a strong-sounding argument is not enough if the data do not justify it. Researchers should also identify limitations, explain uncertainty and avoid overstating what a single study can show. It is important to draw a conclusion that follows from the findings rather than from personal expectations.

Scientific communication allows others to examine and build on a study. Researchers report findings clearly, explain their procedures and submit work to peer review, where other specialists evaluate the quality and significance of the research. Replication can help establish whether a result is consistent under similar conditions. When researchers share methods, provide evidence and remain open to revision, they can advance knowledge and make a significant contribution to a field. Innovation is more trustworthy when it develops from careful inquiry, transparent reporting and conclusions that are proportionate to the evidence.
