How Cities Are Becoming More Liveable

A. On 18 April 2024, the fictional city of Brookdale launched a three-year Liveable Neighbourhoods plan. Urban planner Elena Morris explained that the council wanted to support mixed-use development near existing transport routes rather than allow uncontrolled urban sprawl. A survey of 680 residents found that 54% wanted safer streets and more nearby services.

B. Housing became the plan’s most urgent issue. In a report published on 6 June 2024, housing adviser Malik Turner noted that housing affordability had fallen as rents rose by 12% over two years. The city estimated a housing shortage of 2,400 homes. Its new proposal included affordable housing and support for families experiencing rental pressure, although the report did not promise an immediate fall in rents.

C. Transport engineers began reviewing the public transport network in July. A pilot pedestrian zone opened on Market Street on 2 September 2024, while bus priority lanes aimed to reduce commuter traffic. During the first six weeks, passenger numbers rose by 9%. Transport officer Priya Shah said the figures were encouraging, but further monitoring was needed before drawing conclusions about long-term traffic levels.

D. The plan also included green infrastructure, shared public spaces and energy-efficient homes. On 14 February 2025, local architect James Okafor presented designs for a former warehouse site, combining housing, a library and small businesses. The project could proceed only after the developer received planning permission. Residents asked for more trees and a safe walking route to the nearby school.

E. The council’s review of 30 September 2025 reported that 14 streets had been improved and three parks renovated through urban renewal projects. It concluded that a walkable neighbourhood with reliable transport and useful public amenities could improve quality of life. However, researchers noted that property prices and housing demand would need to be tracked for another two years to see whether the changes benefited all income groups.

7. Odd One Out · Based on SkELL · 10 points

Three options in each question express similar meanings. Choose the one collocation that does NOT belong to the synonym group. All options are natural expressions; the odd one out is different in meaning.

8. Collocation Meaning Check · 10 points

Choose the collocation that matches the meaning described. All options are natural English expressions; use meaning rather than parts of speech.
