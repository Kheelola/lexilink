When Tourism Benefits Visitors and Residents

A. On 5 February 2024, the fictional coastal town of Bellhaven introduced a visitor-management plan after its annual visitor numbers reached 640,000. Tourism officer Sofia Alvarez said the goal was not to discourage travel, but to manage busy periods and protect the town’s heritage site and waterfront. The council asked local businesses to provide clearer information before the summer peak season.

B. Hotel manager Karim Patel reported that many guests appreciated receiving a booking confirmation with transport information and check-in times. A survey of 350 visitors in May found that 81% rated staff customer service positively, while 24% had experienced a travel disruption. The survey did not include every accommodation provider, so the council warned that the results should not be treated as a complete picture of the hospitality sector.

C. The town’s new visitor guide offered a two-day travel itinerary with a guided excursion to a cultural landmark, a market serving local cuisine and a scenic route through the nearby hills. Residents asked that tours avoid narrow streets during school arrival times. Guide organiser Noor Hassan said small groups could improve guest satisfaction while giving visitors more time to learn about local history.

D. To reduce pressure on the waterfront, Bellhaven promoted off-season travel and added an electric shuttle as an example of eco-friendly transport. The council’s 18 October 2024 report said bus use rose by 16% between September and October. It also noted that tourist expenditure varied widely by travel style, and that more data were needed to assess how spending reached family-run businesses.

E. The town’s travel advice now recommends checking room availability, considering travel insurance and following local guidance when visiting protected areas. On 12 March 2025, the council agreed to review responsible tourism measures after another full season. It said visitor numbers alone would not show whether tourism was successful; resident feedback, environmental quality and the survival of local businesses also mattered.

7. Odd One Out · Based on SkELL · 10 points
