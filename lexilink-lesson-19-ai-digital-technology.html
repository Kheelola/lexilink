AI in Everyday Life: Opportunity, Evidence and Responsibility

Artificial intelligence (AI) is already part of everyday digital life. Recommendation systems suggest music and films, navigation tools estimate travel times, and chatbots generate answers to questions. Many systems use algorithms to process large datasets, detect patterns and generate predictions. A developer may train an AI model on examples so that it can recognise images, classify messages or produce content. These tools can enhance productivity, but their results are not automatically accurate or fair.

One challenge is the quality of the data used to train a system. If the examples leave out certain groups or repeat old stereotypes, the system may reproduce that bias. Biased training data can affect decisions about recruitment, lending or access to services. For this reason, developers need to identify a bias, test the system with varied examples and keep human oversight where decisions may affect people’s lives. A polished answer or a confident-looking prediction is not proof that the result is correct.

Users also need to verify a claim and cross-check information before sharing AI-generated content. It is sensible to consult a reliable source, especially when information concerns health, money, law or public events. AI tools can spread misinformation if users repost fabricated content without checking it. Deepfake images and audio may look convincing, so people should ask where an item came from and whether independent evidence supports it. A well-informed user does not assume that every result produced by technology is trustworthy.

Privacy and cybersecurity are equally important. Apps and websites may collect details about location, searches and online behaviour, adding to a person’s digital footprint. Organisations should protect personal data, store data securely and explain how information will be used. If private records are exposed, the incident may become a data breach and compromise privacy. Users can take practical steps: install software updates, use strong passwords and think carefully before granting access to personal information.

Responsible technology requires more than technical performance. Companies should ensure transparency, comply with regulations and implement safeguards that reduce foreseeable harm. A user-friendly interface helps people understand what a tool can and cannot do, while clear explanations make it easier to question an unexpected result. When a system raises concerns or poses a risk, organisations should investigate rather than dismiss the problem. People can benefit from AI while still making an informed decision about when to use it and when to seek human expertise. The aim is not to trust every tool or reject every innovation, but to use digital technology critically, safely and responsibly.

2. Matching · 10 points

Match each beginning with the ending that completes a conventional English collocation. Several options are semantically close, so attend to the word partnership rather than translating each word separately.

3. Complete the Sentence · 10 points

Choose the collocation that best fits the whole situation. The options are intentionally close in meaning; use context and conventional word partnerships to decide.

4. Find the Verb, Find the Collocate · 10 points

Choose the verb that completes each collocation naturally. The verbs may be translated similarly in other languages, but they are not interchangeable in English.

5. Collocation Sorting · 10 points

Select the most precise collocation for each scenario. Use the meaning of the full sentence to distinguish related expressions.

6. Lexical Cloze · 10 points

Word bank: detect patterns · cross-check information · raise concerns · consult a reliable source · human oversight · implement safeguards · digital footprint · ensure transparency · protect personal data · pose a risk

7. Odd One Out · 10 points

Choose the one phrase that is not a natural English collocation. The other three may all be possible in other contexts; focus on the exact wording and meaning in this lesson.

8. Collocation Repair · 10 points

Choose the best correction for a sentence containing an unnatural word partnership or a direct-translation error. The corrected sentence must fit the intended meaning.

9. Interference Challenge · 10 points

Each sentence contains a collocation choice influenced by direct translation. Select the natural English version without changing the intended meaning.

10. Contextual Precision · 10 points

Choose the best collocation for each realistic AI and digital technology scenario. The final items require you to distinguish close meanings, not just identify a familiar phrase.
