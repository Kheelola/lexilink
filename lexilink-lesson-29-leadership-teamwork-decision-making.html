A Project Team Learns to Make Better Decisions

A. On 7 February 2024, the fictional company Northline Services began a six-month team-development project after a staff survey found that 41% of employees were unclear about project priorities. Team director Helen Brooks introduced clear objectives and asked managers to set priorities at the beginning of each week. She explained that leadership should create direction without preventing staff from contributing ideas.

B. Project manager Amir Patel began to delegate tasks according to colleagues’ strengths. The team agreed to keep shared responsibility for the final outcome, even when individual tasks were assigned to different people. A short performance review on 20 April showed that completed weekly tasks had risen from 68% to 82%, although the team noted that the project had also become less complex during that period.

C. Disagreement emerged when a deadline was brought forward. Rather than avoiding the issue, Brooks encouraged colleagues to resolve conflict through a structured decision-making process. Team members compared risks, discussed resource limits and worked to build consensus on a revised schedule. The final agreement was recorded with specific accountability measures so that every person knew which actions they owned.

D. An internal review dated 12 June 2024 found that staff valued constructive feedback but wanted more time for coaching. Human-resources adviser Lina Mensah recommended training managers to motivate staff without relying only on targets. She also suggested that leaders recognise collective effort and encourage employees to take initiative when they notice a problem.

E. The project report published on 9 August 2024 recommended continued strategic planning, realistic ways to manage workload and stronger team cohesion. It noted that a trust-based relationship helped people share concerns early, while effective crisis management depended on calm communication. The report did not claim that one leadership style was best for every situation; it advised managers to adjust their approach to the team and the task.
