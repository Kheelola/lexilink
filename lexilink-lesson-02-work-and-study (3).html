1. Learning outcomes

By the end of the lesson, you will be able to:

  • recognise common collocations related to work, study and career development;
  • retrieve useful lexical chunks as complete units;
  • predict likely word partnerships and check selected predictions in SkELL;
  • use collocations accurately in short workplace and academic contexts.
Lexical principle
Learn words in recurring combinations and useful sentence frames. Notice patterns, retrieve whole chunks and practise adapting them to new contexts.

3. Reading text

Combining Work and Study

Many university students try to balance work and study. A part-time job can help them gain work experience, acquire practical skills and improve their career prospects. At the same time, students must attend lectures, submit assignments and meet deadlines.

Managing these responsibilities requires planning. Students need to take responsibility for their learning and organise their time carefully. When they have a heavy workload, it can be difficult to work under pressure without becoming exhausted. Setting priorities and asking for support can help them manage competing demands.

Work experience can also help students build a career. Employers often look for candidates who meet the requirements of a position, communicate clearly and make a contribution to a team. Professional skills developed at university may therefore be useful in the workplace.

Students do not need to choose between academic development and professional experience in every situation. With realistic planning, they may be able to gain a qualification while developing the skills needed for future employment.

Notice: Which verbs combine with “experience”, “skills”, “a career” and “a deadline”? Remember the whole phrase, not only the individual word.

Part B — SkELL investigation (15 minutes)

Open SkELL: https://skell.sketchengine.eu/

  1. Search one key word: experience, career or skills.
  2. Use the Word Sketch or examples view available for the word. Look for combinations that fit the lesson topic.
  3. Record three collocations you can verify from the displayed data.
  4. Return to your predictions and mark each one CONFIRMED, REVISE or NOT CLEAR FROM THE EXAMPLES.
  5. Choose one useful collocation and copy a short example or note its context.
